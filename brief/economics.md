## Business
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Monthly active users (MAU) | ~2,000,000 (about 9% premium) |
| Total revenue | ~₹4.1 Cr / month |
| Streak Freeze revenue | ~₹45 lakh / month (~11% of revenue). One freeze = ₹49 |
| Premium price | ₹199 / month |
| Value of retention | +1 percentage point of D7 retention across the whole user base ≈ **₹2.6 lakh / month** (Finance rule of thumb) |

## What a wrongly broken streak costs
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Extra churn within 14 days after a user loses a streak of 7+ days (vs users who didn't) | free: **+16 points**; premium cancellations: **+12 points** |
| Remaining value of a user we keep | free: **₹45**; premium: **₹1,600** |
| Users who file a ticket after a streak breaks that they believe they kept | **35%** |
| Cost of one support ticket | **₹40** |

## What a fake streak day costs
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Lost freeze sale | about **20%** of users who backfill a missed day would otherwise have bought a ₹49 freeze |
| Integrity cost (leaderboards, certificates, trust in the streak) | Farhan: "call it **₹5** per fake day. It's probably higher, but I can't prove it." |

## "I was offline" late syncs we can't verify (Trust & Safety)
At 2M MAU we see about **3,000 per day** of uploads that look like u13 / u14: a single day's lesson arriving in one batch about a day late, for a lesson that was already published at the claimed time, right after the user opens the app online.
- About **60%** of them would otherwise break a streak of 7+ days.
- About **10%** of them are premium users.
- T&S manually audited 200 similar cases where hard proof *was* possible (the "watched" lesson was published after the claimed time): **23 (11.5%)** were clock tampering. That method only catches people who happened to pick a brand-new lesson, so the true share is probably higher. Farhan's guess: up to **25%**.
